Things to do in Rome

Step into the Colosseum

The Colosseum is Rome's most iconic monument and the largest amphitheater ever built, capable of holding up to 80,000 spectators in ancient times. This architectural marvel has stood for nearly 2,000 years, hosting gladiatorial contests, animal hunts, and mock naval battles. Walking through the underground chambers where gladiators and beasts once waited reveals the sophisticated engineering behind the spectacles that entertained Roman citizens.

A combined ticket grants access to the Colosseum, Roman Forum, and Palatine Hill - the heart of ancient Rome where emperors lived in lavish palaces overlooking the city. Book skip-the-line tickets online and consider an early morning or late afternoon visit to avoid crowds and harsh midday sun. Guided tours bring the ruins to life with stories of political intrigue, military triumphs, and daily life in the ancient empire. The Arch of Constantine nearby commemorates the emperor's victory and displays intricate relief sculptures chronicling his achievements.

Marvel at Vatican City

Vatican City, the world's smallest independent state, houses extraordinary artistic and religious treasures within its walls. St. Peter's Basilica dominates the skyline with Michelangelo's magnificent dome, visible from across Rome. Inside, masterpieces include Michelangelo's Pietà, Bernini's bronze baldachin, and lavishly decorated chapels. Climb the 551 steps to the dome's top for breathtaking views over Vatican Gardens and Rome's terracotta rooftops.

The Vatican Museums contain one of the world's greatest art collections, culminating in the Sistine Chapel where Michelangelo's ceiling frescoes and Last Judgment leave visitors speechless. The Raphael Rooms, Gallery of Maps with stunning cartographic frescoes, and the ancient sculpture collections including the Laocoön Group require several hours to appreciate fully. Book tickets months in advance, dress modestly (covering shoulders and knees), and arrive early to beat tour group rushes. Wednesday mornings offer the chance to attend the Pope's general audience in St. Peter's Square, requiring free tickets booked in advance.

Discover Trastevere Nightlife

Trastevere is Rome's most charming neighborhood, with medieval narrow streets, ivy-draped buildings, and a bohemian atmosphere that comes alive after sunset. Unlike tourist-heavy areas, Trastevere maintains authentic Roman character where locals gather at family-run trattorias serving traditional recipes passed down through generations. The cobblestone piazzas fill with street musicians, artists, and Romans enjoying aperitivo hour at outdoor tables.

Start your evening at Santa Maria in Trastevere, one of Rome's oldest churches with stunning gold mosaics that glow at dusk. Wander through the neighborhood's winding alleys discovering artisan workshops, vintage boutiques, and hidden bars serving innovative cocktails alongside classic Roman wines. Try authentic cacio e pepe or amatriciana at a local osteria, then enjoy gelato while people-watching from the fountain steps. The neighborhood transforms as night deepens, with jazz clubs, wine bars, and clubs keeping the energy high until dawn. Cross the Tiber River for magical nighttime views of illuminated bridges and domes reflecting in the water.
