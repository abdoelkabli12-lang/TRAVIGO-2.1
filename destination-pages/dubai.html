Things to do in Dubai

Ascend Burj Khalifa

The Burj Khalifa pierces the sky at 828 meters (2,717 feet), making it the world's tallest building and Dubai's most iconic landmark. The observation decks on the 124th, 125th, and 148th floors offer breathtaking 360-degree views across the desert cityscape, Persian Gulf coastline, and endless sand dunes stretching to the horizon. High-speed elevators whisk visitors up at 10 meters per second, and the multimedia displays explain Dubai's transformation from fishing village to futuristic metropolis in just decades.

Book tickets well in advance, especially for the premium Sky Lounge on the 148th floor offering luxury seating, refreshments, and fewer crowds. Sunset provides the best views as the city transitions from day to dusk, with lights gradually illuminating the urban landscape below. The nightly fountain show at the base, choreographed to music with water jets reaching 150 meters high, is best viewed from nearby restaurants. Visit the Dubai Mall connected to the tower for luxury shopping, an Olympic-sized ice rink, and a massive aquarium with walk-through tunnel. The entire Downtown Dubai area represents the city's ambition and innovation, creating a vertical city within a city.

Experience Desert Safari Adventure

A desert safari is quintessential Dubai - leaving the ultramodern city to experience the vast Arabian desert where Bedouin culture thrived for centuries. The adventure begins with thrilling dune bashing in 4x4 vehicles, racing up and down massive sand dunes in an adrenaline-pumping roller coaster ride across golden landscapes. Skilled drivers navigate the challenging terrain, creating an unforgettable experience as the sun casts long shadows across rippling sand patterns.

After the excitement, arrive at a traditional Bedouin-style camp for cultural immersion. Try sandboarding down dunes, ride camels as the sun sets painting the sky in brilliant oranges and pinks, and get henna tattoos applied by local artists. The evening features a delicious BBQ dinner with Arabic mezze, grilled meats, and traditional sweets served under starlit skies. Entertainment includes mesmerizing belly dancing, Tanoura spinning performances with colorful skirts, and fire shows. Upgrade to overnight camping to sleep in desert tents, wake to sunrise over dunes, and experience the profound silence of the desert night. Some tours offer falconry demonstrations and opportunities to hold these magnificent birds of prey, symbols of Emirati heritage.

Shop at Traditional Souks

Dubai's traditional souks (markets) in the historic Al Fahidi district offer a stark contrast to gleaming modern malls, preserving the trading heritage that built this city. The Gold Souk dazzles with shop windows displaying elaborate necklaces, bracelets, and ornate jewelry crafted from gold, diamonds, and precious gems. Hundreds of shops compete for business, and bargaining is expected - start at half the asking price and negotiate. The souk sells jewelry by weight, with craftsmanship charges added, offering better value than Western retailers.

Adjacent to the Gold Souk, the Spice Souk fills narrow covered alleys with aromatic towers of saffron, cardamom, frankincense, dried lemons, and exotic spices shipped from across the Middle East, Africa, and Asia. Vendors eagerly offer samples and explain uses for unfamiliar ingredients. The nearby Textile Souk sells colorful fabrics, pashminas, Persian carpets, and traditional Emirati clothing. Cross Dubai Creek on traditional wooden abra water taxis (just 1 dirham) for an authentic experience, watching cargo dhows being loaded as they have been for generations. The creek area reveals Dubai's trading history before oil wealth transformed it. Evening brings cooler temperatures perfect for souk exploring, when locals emerge for shopping and the areas buzz with energy and negotiation.
